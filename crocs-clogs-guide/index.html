Crocs makes a lot more than one clog. There are platforms, cozy lined pairs, recovery clogs, closed work clogs, and kids' sizes, and each one fits a little differently. Here's what each main style is for, so you can skip the guesswork.

How we wrote this: we haven't wear-tested every style. This guide is based on Crocs' own product and fit information and the current Amazon listings, with sources linked. Fit notes are quoted from each listing because they differ by style.

First, how Crocs sizing works

Crocs describes three fits in its fit guide (Crocs):

  • Roomy: the widest and longest fit, like the original clog. Your feet often won't touch the sides.
  • Relaxed: a little room, but more secure than roomy.
  • Standard: snug but not tight, with little or no slipping.

The Amazon listings give style-by-style advice, and it doesn't all point the same way. Some styles say size down and others say size up, so read the fit note for the exact style you want.

The everyday clogs

Crocs Classic Clog

The one that started it all. Crocs says it's light, water-friendly, easy to clean, and has pivoting heel straps. Its product page says a pair "holds 26 Jibbitz" (Crocs).

Best for: errands, the beach, the garden, and just about everything else.

  • Fit note (listing): "Runs large. Size down if between sizes."

Crocband and Bayaband

These are the sporty versions with a stripe around the sole. The Crocband listing recommends sizing up. The Bayaband adds perforated detail and takes Jibbitz.

Baya

The Baya is a twist on the Classic with extra ventilation. Its listing says it helps drain water and debris, and it recommends sizing down.

The cozy clog

Crocs Classic Lined Clog

This is the Classic with a fuzzy liner, and it works as a slipper that can handle quick errands. The listing says it's roomy and suggests ordering up to the next whole size. We cover it in depth in our Classic Lined review.

Best for: chilly mornings and house-shoe duty.

The platforms

Crocs Classic Platform Clog

This is the Classic upper on a 1.6-inch (41mm) wedge, according to the listing, and it holds charms on the backstrap too.

  • Fit note (listing): "Runs small. Size up if between sizes."

Want more height? The Crush Clog is listed with a 2-inch height, and that listing recommends sizing up. See our platform clog guide for more options.

The comfort-first styles

  • Echo Clog: a sporty clog with a 1.5-inch height boost, a LiteRide drop-in footbed, and a padded heel strap. The listing says it runs large.
  • Mellow Recovery Clog: deep, cupped footbeds with "sink-in" softness. The listing calls the fit roomy and recommends sizing down.
  • Dylan Clog: a Crocs take on the leather mule, with deep heel cups. The listing says it runs large.

The work clogs

Crocs On The Clock Clog

A closed toe and heel plus Crocs Lock slip-resistant tread, built for kitchens and hospitals. The listing says it runs large. The closed-toe Bistro is another option, with extra coverage over the top of the foot. Always check your employer's footwear rules. See our nurses' clog guide for more.

Best for: shifts where vent holes aren't allowed.

Care in one line

Rinse or wipe with mild soap, air dry, and keep Crocs away from high heat like hot cars, which Crocs says can shrink or warp them (Crocs FAQ).

Which Crocs should you get?

  • One pair for everything: Classic Clog.
  • Cold feet: Classic Lined.
  • A little lift: Classic Platform, or the Crush for more.
  • Tired feet after a long day: Mellow Recovery.
  • Work shifts: On The Clock.
