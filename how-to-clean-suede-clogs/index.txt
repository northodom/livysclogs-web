17:T17cf,<p>Suede clogs are the coziest-looking shoes of fall. They&#39;re also the ones that make you wince when you step in a puddle. The good news: suede is easier to care for than it seems, as long as you go gently and skip a few common mistakes.</p>
<p><em>This guide is based on care advice from shoe brands (mainly Birkenstock) and published care coverage, with sources linked. Always check the care tag or brand instructions for your exact pair.</em></p>
<h2 id="what-youll-need">What you&#39;ll need</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>A suede or nubuck brush</strong> (many have a rubber side and a bristle side)</li>
<li><strong>A suede eraser</strong> for small marks and shiny spots</li>
<li><strong>A clean, lint-free cloth</strong></li>
<li><strong>A colorless suede protector or waterproofing spray</strong></li>
<li><strong>Paper towels or plain paper</strong> for stuffing the clogs while they dry</li>
</ul>
<p>Suede care kits that bundle a brush, eraser, and spray are easy to find. Whatever you choose, test any cleaner or spray on a small hidden spot first, because some products can darken suede.</p>
<h2 id="step-1-let-dirt-dry-first">Step 1: Let dirt dry first</h2>
<p>If your clogs get muddy, don&#39;t scrub right away. Wet mud smears deeper into the nap. Let it dry completely at room temperature, then brush it off. One detailed Boston review recommends the same approach: let mud dry, brush, then use an eraser on marks (<a href="https://shoestoryteller.com/birkenstock-boston-clogs-review/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Shoe Storyteller</a>).</p>
<h2 id="step-2-brush-to-lift-dust-and-restore-the-nap">Step 2: Brush to lift dust and restore the nap</h2>
<p>Birkenstock recommends removing dirt and dust with a rubber nubuck brush and/or a lint-free cloth, and brushing the upper regularly to keep suede looking its best (<a href="https://www.birkenstock.com/us/faq-how-do-i-care-for-the-upper-material-of-my-birkenstock-shoes.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Birkenstock care FAQ</a>).</p>
<ul>
<li>Brush in short, light strokes.</li>
<li>Go with the grain first, then lightly against it to lift flattened areas.</li>
<li>Don&#39;t press hard. You want to fluff the fibers, not wear them down.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="step-3-erase-small-marks-and-shiny-spots">Step 3: Erase small marks and shiny spots</h2>
<p>For scuffs, small stains, or shiny worn patches (common where the strap rubs), rub a suede eraser gently back and forth over the spot, then brush away the crumbs. For stubborn marks, you can apply a bit more pressure, but work slowly.</p>
<h2 id="step-4-handle-water-stains-and-wet-clogs-carefully">Step 4: Handle water stains and wet clogs carefully</h2>
<p>Got caught in the rain? Don&#39;t panic, and <strong>don&#39;t use heat.</strong></p>
<ol>
<li>Blot extra water with a clean cloth. Don&#39;t rub.</li>
<li>Stuff the clogs loosely with paper to help them keep their shape.</li>
<li>Let them dry at room temperature, away from radiators, heaters, hair dryers, and direct sun. Birkenstock&#39;s care advice repeatedly says to dry at room temperature, and the same Boston review warns that cork can swell if water soaks the footbed sidewall.</li>
<li>Once fully dry, brush the nap back up.</li>
</ol>
<h2 id="step-5-protect-before-and-after-you-wear-them">Step 5: Protect before (and after) you wear them</h2>
<p>Birkenstock recommends treating suede regularly with a colorless waterproofing spray (<a href="https://www.birkenstock.com/us/faq-how-do-i-care-for-the-upper-material-of-my-birkenstock-shoes.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Birkenstock care FAQ</a>). A good routine:</p>
<ul>
<li>Spray new clogs before the first wear, following the product&#39;s directions.</li>
<li>Let them dry completely before wearing.</li>
<li>Re-apply every so often, and especially after they&#39;ve been out in wet weather.</li>
</ul>
<p>Spray outside or in a well-ventilated room, and keep the spray off the cork and footbed unless the product says it&#39;s meant for them.</p>
<h2 id="what-not-to-do">What NOT to do</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Don&#39;t machine wash suede clogs.</strong></li>
<li><strong>Don&#39;t soak them</strong> or hold them under running water.</li>
<li><strong>Don&#39;t dry them with heat.</strong> Dry slowly at room temperature, never near a radiator or heater (<a href="https://shoestoryteller.com/birkenstock-boston-clogs-review/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Shoe Storyteller</a>).</li>
<li><strong>Don&#39;t use harsh household cleaners</strong> or bleach wipes on suede.</li>
<li><strong>Don&#39;t scrub aggressively.</strong> Gentle and patient wins.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="a-quick-note-on-lined-and-foam-clogs">A quick note on lined and foam clogs</h2>
<p>Not every cozy clog is suede. For a fuzzy-lined foam clog like the Crocs Classic Lined, Crocs has its own care guidance (mild soap, air dry, no heat). We cover it in our Crocs Classic Lined Clog review. Leather work clogs like the Dansko Professional are simpler still: Dansko says to wipe leather with a damp cloth, and use soap and water for tougher marks (<a href="https://dansko.com/products/professional-clog-tortoise-patent" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Dansko</a>).</p>
<h2 id="suede-clogs-worth-caring-for">Suede clogs worth caring for</h2>
<p>If you&#39;re shopping for a suede pair, these are the ones we&#39;d look at:</p>
<ul>
<li><a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B000GLNXCA?tag=livysclogs-20" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener" target="_blank">Birkenstock Boston Soft Footbed (Suede)</a>: the classic. Read our Boston review.</li>
<li><a href="https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0CLHBJ4V8?tag=livysclogs-20" rel="nofollow sponsored noopener" target="_blank">CUSHIONAIRE Granola Suede Platform Clog</a>: a chunky suede platform. See our platform clog picks.</li>
</ul>
<p>A few minutes of brushing each week and a fresh coat of protector each season will keep your suede clogs looking cozy for a long time.</p>
