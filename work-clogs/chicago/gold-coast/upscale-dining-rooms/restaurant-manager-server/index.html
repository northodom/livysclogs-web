Working the room on the Gold Coast

Choose Chicago, the city's official tourism organization, calls the Gold Coast "a luxury lover's haven," with "charming homes on picture-perfect streets, high-end boutiques, and restaurants favored by celebrities past and present." It places the neighborhood close to the Lake Michigan shoreline and describes streets "lined with historic mansions, designer boutiques, celebrated restaurants, and historic hotels" (Choose Chicago).

The evenings have their own rhythm. Choose Chicago says that in the evenings, "a dynamic bar-hopping scene" spills out onto Rush Street "until late into the night." It also calls the Gold Coast "an architectural jewel box," pointing to the Astor Street District's rows of 19th-century homes, and it lists the Newberry Library on Washington Square and the Porchlight Music Theatre among the things to see.

Choose Chicago also points to the Charnley-Persky House, designed by Louis Sullivan and Frank Lloyd Wright, and to the International Museum of Surgical Science, housed in a lakefront mansion. For restaurant staff, a neighborhood of historic hotels, boutiques, and a Rush Street bar scene that runs late can mean front-of-house shifts that stretch well into the evening.

The Gold Coast sits within Chicago's Near North Side community area, according to the City Colleges of Chicago's community research guide (CCC).

We won't guess at how any particular Gold Coast dining room runs. Still, when a neighborhood is known for celebrated restaurants and historic hotels, front-of-house staff are working in full view of guests. That makes the look of a work shoe part of the decision along with comfort and grip. If your restaurant has a dress code, read it before you shop: it may name a color, a material, or a style of shoe.

The manager's view

A restaurant manager's night can move in every direction. The work might include walking the floor before doors open, checking reservations, stepping in at the host stand, answering a question at the bar, and looking in at the pass when a table is waiting. Some of it is brisk walking and some is standing still while watching the room.

For a manager, the shoe has to hold up across all of that and still look tidy at the end of the night. If there is a dress code, the manager may be the one explaining it to new hires, so it helps when the manager's own shoes meet it.

The server's view

A server measures a shift in steps: table to terminal, terminal to bar, bar back to the table, with plates, glassware, or a tray in hand. Standing time adds up too, at tableside, during side work, and while waiting on an order.

Depending on the building, those steps may cross carpet, wood, stone, or tile, and the route may pass through the kitchen or a dish area. Ask where your route goes. If it includes kitchen floors, grip matters as much as looks.

What to look for in a front-of-house work clog

Start with your restaurant's dress code and footwear rule, then compare against this list.

  • A closed heel. A closed-back clog wraps the heel instead of leaving it open, unlike mule-style clogs. The pick below is closed-back.
  • A polished, easy-care upper. Smooth materials that wipe clean make it easier to keep shoes looking neat from one service to the next.
  • The right sole for your route. If your shift crosses kitchen or dish-area floors, look for a maker that names a slip-resistance standard, and match it to anything your employer requires. ASTM F2913 is one test you may see; ASTM describes it as a lab test method that measures friction between a shoe and a floor to evaluate relative slip performance (ASTM). A named test explains how grip was measured, not that a shoe can't slip.
  • Support for walking and standing. Look for a shaped footbed and read what the maker says the shoe is built for.
  • Fit that suits your foot. Clog fit differs from sneaker fit, and each maker gives its own guidance.

This page isn't medical advice. If long shifts leave your feet or back sore, talk with a podiatrist.

Front-of-house questions

Why does a Gold Coast manager need the same shoe as a server? They don't have to wear the same pair, but they walk the same dining room and answer to the same dress code if there is one. That's why this page uses one checklist, and one clog, for both jobs.

Do servers need a slip-resistant shoe? That depends on your route and your employer. If your shifts take you into the kitchen or a dish area, or your employer names a slip standard, choose a shoe whose maker states one. If you're unsure, ask your manager.

Can a work clog pass a dress code? It depends on the code. Compare the listing's photos, colors, and materials with your restaurant's rules, and ask before you buy if the code is strict.

Should a clog feel loose at the heel? Dansko says yes, within limits: your heel should move up and down freely as you walk, and your toes shouldn't touch the front. Read Dansko's fit notes before ordering.
