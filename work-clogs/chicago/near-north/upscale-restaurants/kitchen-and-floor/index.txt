1a:T2805,<p><strong>Two perspectives. Same shoes.</strong> In an upscale restaurant, the kitchen and the dining room run on the same clock but on different floors. The cook works the line and the server works the room, and both are on their feet for the whole shift. This guide covers what a work clog needs to do on each side of the kitchen door in Near North, and lands on one closed-heel clog for both: the Dansko Professional in Black Patent.</p>

</ul>
<h2 id="working-in-near-north">Working in Near North</h2>
<p>On this site, Near North means Old Town and the blocks around it. It sits apart from our Gold Coast page, which covers the dining rooms of the Gold Coast itself. The City Colleges of Chicago&#39;s community research guide lists Old Town among the neighborhoods of Chicago&#39;s Near North Side community area (<a href="https://researchguides.ccc.edu/community_survey/Central" rel="noopener" target="_blank">CCC</a>).</p>
<p>Choose Chicago, the city&#39;s official tourism organization, says it loves Old Town &quot;for its vintage charm, beautiful boutiques, and late-night buzz.&quot; It calls Wells Street &quot;the main drag where you&#39;ll find tons of buzzing spots to eat and drink,&quot; and describes Old Town as &quot;the birthplace of modern American improv&quot; (<a href="https://www.choosechicago.com/neighborhoods/old-town/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Choose Chicago</a>).</p>
<p>For restaurant staff, a neighborhood known for late-night buzz can mean shifts that run well into the evening. We won&#39;t guess at how any particular Near North restaurant runs, so start with your own employer&#39;s rules. If your restaurant has a dress code or a footwear rule, read it before you shop: it may name a color, a material, or a style of shoe, or require a shoe whose maker makes a slip claim.</p>
<h2 id="the-kitchens-view">The kitchen&#39;s view</h2>
<p>A cook&#39;s shift can start with prep and end with breaking down the station. In between there is standing at the line, turning between the stove and the pass, and walking to storage and the dish area. Kitchen floors can be hard, and they can get wet or greasy during service.</p>
<p>For the kitchen, the questions are support for long hours of standing and what the sole is made to do. If your kitchen has a footwear rule, it usually decides the sole question for you.</p>
<h2 id="the-dining-rooms-view">The dining room&#39;s view</h2>
<p>A server measures a shift in steps: table to terminal, terminal to bar, and back through the kitchen door to pick up plates. Standing adds up too, at tableside, during side work and while waiting on an order.</p>
<p>In the dining room, the shoe is part of the look. Guests see it, and a dress code may name it. The route may still cross the kitchen or dish area, so ask where yours goes. If it includes kitchen floors, grip matters as much as looks.</p>
<h2 id="what-to-look-for-in-a-work-clog-for-both-sides-of-the-door">What to look for in a work clog for both sides of the door</h2>
<p>Start with your restaurant&#39;s dress code and footwear rule, then compare against this list.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>A closed heel.</strong> A closed-back clog wraps the heel instead of leaving it open, unlike mule-style clogs. The pick below is closed-back.</li>
<li><strong>A polished, easy-care upper.</strong> Smooth materials that wipe clean make it easier to keep shoes looking neat from one service to the next, and to clean up after a kitchen shift.</li>
<li><strong>The right sole for your route.</strong> If your shift crosses kitchen or dish-area floors, look for a maker that names a slip-resistance standard, and match it to anything your employer requires. ASTM F2913 is one test you may see; ASTM describes it as a lab test method that measures friction between a shoe and a floor to evaluate relative slip performance (<a href="https://www.astm.org/f2913-19.html" rel="noopener" target="_blank">ASTM</a>). A named test explains how grip was measured, not that a shoe can&#39;t slip.</li>
<li><strong>Support for walking and standing.</strong> Look for a shaped footbed and read what the maker says the shoe is built for.</li>
<li><strong>Fit that suits your foot.</strong> Clog fit differs from sneaker fit, and each maker gives its own guidance.</li>
</ul>
<p><em>This page isn&#39;t medical advice. If long shifts leave your feet or back sore, talk with a podiatrist.</em></p>

<p>Both views on this page end at the same pair. The cook and the server work the same service on either side of one door, so this page puts the same Dansko Professional in Black Patent on both of them.</p>

<h3 id="upper-toe-box-and-heel">Upper, toe box and heel</h3>
<p>Dansko describes the Black Patent upper this way: &quot;Patent leather that&#39;s easy to wipe clean.&quot; Its care note says to gently wipe surface dirt off with a clean damp cloth, use soap and water for a tougher blemish, and dry with a soft, dry cloth. Dansko also lists:</p>
<ul>
<li>&quot;Roomy, reinforced toe box.&quot; Dansko says it lets toes move comfortably, with plenty of &quot;wiggle room&quot;</li>

<h3 id="dimensions-width-and-fit">Dimensions, width and fit</h3>
<p>Dansko gives a heel height of 2 inches and a platform of 0.8 inches. That&#39;s not a flat shoe, so try it at home before a full shift. On Dansko&#39;s page for this colorway, Regular is the only width shown.</p>
<p>Dansko&#39;s page says its shoes &quot;are sized according to European (EU) whole size standards,&quot; so check Dansko&#39;s women&#39;s size chart before you pick a size. Dansko&#39;s fit notes on the same page say the fit may be slightly snug when you step in, your toes shouldn&#39;t touch the front, and your heel should move up and down freely as you walk.</p>
<h3 id="the-sole-and-your-route">The sole and your route</h3>
<p><strong>Dansko makes no slip-resistance claim for the Professional</strong>, and neither do we. If your route crosses kitchen or dish-area floors, or your restaurant&#39;s footwear rule calls for a shoe whose maker makes a slip claim, check the rule before you buy, and choose a shoe whose maker makes that claim if you need one.</p>
<h3 id="from-the-kitchens-side">From the kitchen&#39;s side</h3>
<p>Dansko makes no slip claim for this clog, so a cook whose kitchen requires a slip-resistant shoe should check that rule first. Where the rule allows it, Dansko describes a closed back, a contoured footbed and an upper that&#39;s easy to wipe clean after service.</p>
<h3 id="from-the-dining-rooms-side">From the dining room&#39;s side</h3>
<p>Guests see a server&#39;s shoes all night, and Dansko describes this upper as black patent leather that&#39;s easy to wipe clean. Check it against your restaurant&#39;s dress code.</p>

<h2 id="kitchen-and-floor-questions">Kitchen and floor questions</h2>
<p><strong>Can a cook and a server wear the same clog?</strong>
They don&#39;t have to, but they work the same service and answer to the same restaurant&#39;s rules. This page uses one checklist, and one clog, for both jobs. The one difference to check is the sole: if the kitchen requires a slip-resistant shoe, this clog&#39;s maker doesn&#39;t make that claim.</p>
<p><strong>Do I need a slip-resistant shoe?</strong>
That depends on your route and your employer. If your shifts take you onto kitchen or dish-area floors, or your employer names a slip standard, choose a shoe whose maker states one. If you&#39;re unsure, ask your manager or chef.</p>
<p><strong>Can a work clog pass a dress code?</strong>
It depends on the code. Compare the listing&#39;s photos, colors and materials with your restaurant&#39;s rules, and ask before you buy if the code is strict.</p>
<p><strong>Should a clog feel loose at the heel?</strong>
Dansko says yes, within limits: your heel should move up and down freely as you walk, and your toes shouldn&#39;t touch the front. Read Dansko&#39;s fit notes before ordering.</p>
