At the pass: what the chef needs

The chef is the one who keeps moving. She checks the walk-in, tastes sauces, calls tickets at the pass and visits every station more than once a night. Her route crosses whatever the floor has on it: water near the dish pit, a slick spot by the fryers, cold concrete in the cooler.

That's why a chef often cares about two things in a shoe: whether it keeps her comfortable past the tenth hour, and whether it fits the house footwear rule she may have to enforce for everyone else.

On the line: what the line cook needs

The line cook barely leaves her post. Sauté, grill or garde manger, her world is a small patch of tile between burners, cutting board and lowboy fridge. On a heavy night she turns in place hundreds of times, with a cook on each side and hot pans within reach.

Spills build up on that patch as service goes on, and the closing scrub-down leaves it wet again. For her, the clog has to feel steady underfoot hour after hour, keep her toes from being squeezed, and hold up to a leather-care routine she can actually keep.

What to look for in a women's kitchen clog

Start with your kitchen's footwear rule. Once you know it, use these points to narrow the listings.

  • What the maker says about grip. If your job requires a slip-resistant shoe, look for a maker who states a slip claim or names the test used, and match it to the requirement. Even a tested sole can slip.
  • Closed back, closed front. A closed heel stays with you on turns, and a covered foot is protected from splashes. If you need a safety toe, only consider listings that state a safety-toe rating.
  • Leather you can look after. Leather uppers need their own care routine. Read the maker's care note before you buy, so you know what upkeep you're signing up for.
  • Arch support for standing. A contoured footbed with built-in arch support is worth looking for when you stand all shift.
  • Women's sizing and width. Buy from the women's size chart, and check whether the colorway comes in a wide width if you need one.

Nothing here is medical advice. See a podiatrist if shifts leave your feet aching or you have a condition such as plantar fasciitis.

The sole, and the footwear rule

On the Honey Distressed page, Dansko calls the outsole a PU rocker bottom that "promotes forward motion for shock absorption and energy return," and says its wide heel strike "assists with stability." You won't find a slip claim from Dansko for this model, and we don't make one either. If your employer's footwear rule asks for a shoe that the maker says is slip-rated, this clog doesn't meet that on the maker's word, so pick a different shoe.

The Honey Distressed leather

Honey Distressed is a full-grain leather, and Dansko says it has been buffed and tumbled to give it a worn look. Dansko's summary of the leather is: "Leather that holds up, protects and gets better with age." For care, Dansko says: "To clean, use a soft dry cloth." It also recommends its Beeswax Conditioner before the first wear and every couple of weeks after, to maintain, moisturize and weatherproof the leather.

Toe box, collar and heel

Dansko's spec list for this colorway includes:

  • "Roomy, reinforced toe box allows toes to move comfortably with plenty of "wiggle room.""
  • a padded instep collar and a leather socklining, which Dansko says is there for breathability
  • a heel counter that lets the heel rise and fall freely while giving side-to-side stability
  • an inner frame that "provides added stability throughout"

Arch support and footbed

Dansko describes the contoured footbed as "Built-in natural arch support for all-day comfort." The footbed is single-density open-cell PU foam, and the product is listed as APMA Accepted on the same page.

Height and widths

Dansko lists a 2-inch heel and a 0.8-inch platform, so this is a raised shoe; wear it around the house before your first shift. Dansko's Honey Distressed page shows two widths, Regular and Wide. Whether the Amazon listing has Wide in your size is something to check there.

Getting the fit right

Dansko sizes its shoes to "European (EU) whole size standards," so use Dansko's women's size chart. According to Dansko's fit guidance, the clog can feel a little snug as you step in, your heel should rise and fall freely while you walk, and your toes should never press against the front. Standing, you should have about a pinky finger's width behind your heel. More detail is in our Dansko Professional fit guide.
