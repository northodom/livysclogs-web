Behind the pass: the chef

A chef rarely stands still for long. Expediting, checking plates, stepping into the cooler for a count and walking the line to see how each cook is keeping up, she may cross the kitchen dozens of times in one service. She's often the person who hands a new hire the dress code, shoes included.

So she tends to judge a clog on two questions: will it still feel good in the twelfth hour, and does it pass the restaurant's own footwear policy?

At the station: the line cook

A line cook's territory is a few tiles wide. Range, board and reach-in are an arm's length apart, and during a push she rotates between them nonstop while the cooks on either side do the same.

Her checklist for a clog is short: enough space up front so her toes aren't cramped and leather care that's realistic after a late close.

Buying a women's kitchen clog: what to weigh

The restaurant's footwear policy comes first. With it in hand, compare listings on these points.

  • Written maker claims about the sole. If the policy calls for a specific sole rating, choose a maker that states that rating, and compare it with the policy. No rating makes a fall impossible.
  • A back that holds the heel, a front that covers the foot. Closed-back clogs keep the heel in place as you turn. For safety-toe requirements, consider only listings that state a safety-toe rating.
  • Leather care that fits your routine. Different leathers call for different care, and the maker's care note tells you which.
  • Arch support you can stand on. For shifts on your feet, look for a contoured footbed with arch support built in.
  • Women's sizing in the right width. Size from the women's chart, and confirm the maker offers your width in the color you want.

We don't give medical advice. A podiatrist is the right person to see if your feet hurt after shifts or you have a condition like plantar fasciitis.

Arch support

The Black Box page's Contoured Footbed note promises "Built-in natural arch support for all-day comfort." Dansko gives the footbed material as "Single-density open-cell PU foam," and APMA Accepted is one of the page's four Key Details.

Measurements and widths

For Black Box, Dansko lists a heel height of 2 in and a platform height of 0.8 in. Walk around at home for an evening before the first shift, since the lift is noticeable. The width options on Dansko's Black Box page are Regular and Wide; whether your width is in stock is something to confirm on Amazon.

Fit notes

Dansko's Black Box page explains that its shoes follow "European (EU) whole size standards," so pick your size from Dansko's women's chart. Its closed-back fit guidance says to expect that the clog may be "slightly snug when stepping in," your toes shouldn't reach the front, and your heel should lift freely with each step. When standing, Dansko says to look for "about a pinky-finger's width of space between your heel and the back of the shoe." For more, see our Dansko Professional fit guide.
